◐ Policy

A 429 used up a delivery's retries

An ops-notifier delivery (/984, payment.failed) got 429 on all six attempts and was dead-lettered. A 429 means "slow down", not "failed". The pacer already slows down for it, but each 429 still counts as one of the six attempts. 1,398 ops-notifier 429s this run, so this will happen more at higher rates.

Growth by tens couldn't keep up with the first seconds

Why the ramp is the whole problem. A resize only adds 10 lanes, and the next one waits until permits catch up with lanes again, about 0.15–0.45 s per step. Doubling from 5 reaches 320 in 6 steps (about 1–3 s), and 640 in 7. Growing by the backlog (for example "enough lanes for what's waiting") would get there in one or two. The final lane counts also show overshoot. Analytics needs about 150 lanes for 3,000 a second at 50 ms, and fraud about 160 for its steady 1,075 a second, but they ended at 435 and 495, because permits keep climbing while a backlog exists.

As predicted, scheduler overhead shrinks as load rises (34% → 26%, with the thread wake-up call alone going 12% → 8%), because each wake-up finds more work. Lane workers' share grows, since they now do more of the actual sending. The retry helpers' share fell. Your own locks and map scans are still under 2%. At this rate the machine isn't close to a limit. The limits in this run were all in the design: ramp speed, queue size, the permit ceiling.

Next

Three fixes, then run this again

  1. Grow fast: doubling, or by the backlog

    This is the fix for all 1,604 refusals. With doubling, both endpoints would have had their 160–320 lanes within a couple of seconds, inside what the 200-slot queue can absorb. Keep the 500 ceiling in mind: the fraud vendor reached it, and doubling from 320 would jump past it, so the cap needs handling.

  2. Don't let 429s use up attempts

    Treat a 429 as "wait and retry" without counting it towards the six attempts (or count it separately with a much higher limit). A rate-limited delivery shouldn't be dead-lettered.

  3. Think about admission for analytics

    Even with fast growth, a sudden jump could fill the 200-slot queue again. For an endpoint that must never lose events, refusing at admission is the wrong outcome. A bigger queue, or holding in a waiting list instead of refusing, would keep the "never lose" promise.

  4. Then repeat this exact run

    Same trace, same rate. Success means 0 refused, 0 rate-limit dead letters, and a fraud-vendor p99 under 2 s.
