What 5 starting lanes changed

Merchant backends barely changed. With 5 lanes, two unrelated deliveries share a lane more often, and the second waits behind a 100 ms request: deliveries that waited over 50 ms went from 59 to 140 of 10,501, and p99 from 1 ms to 66 ms. The ERPs finished at t+101 s instead of t+86 s. That's probably the same effect with 5-second requests, but it varies from run to run, so treat it as a hint.

Next

Grow faster, then raise the rate

  1. Double instead of adding 10

    From 5, steps of +10 took 10–12 resizes to reach 105–125. Doubling (5 → 10 → 20 → 40 → 80 → 160) gets there in 5. That shortens the time under capacity that cost the fraud vendor its tail. Doubling overshoots more, so keep the 500-permit ceiling.

  2. The helpers are now the biggest share

    Each endpoint has a dispatcher, a retry helper waking every 100 ms, and a logger, which is 3,426 goroutines and about 11,000 retry-helper wake-ups a second, mostly finding nothing. That's not urgent, but it's the next place the count can come down, for example with one shared logger, or a retry helper that sleeps until the earliest retryAt.

  3. Step the rate up

    At 785/s the system now has room to spare. Batch the generator per tick for 1,200/s and then 3,000/s, with a longer trace read while sending.
