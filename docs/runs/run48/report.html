Why 40 lanes means a fixed ceiling. Each lane sends one request at a time, so an endpoint's throughput can't exceed lanes ÷ time per request. For analytics that's 40 ÷ 50 ms = 800/s. Its pace is also 50 ms per lane, so 50 ms is the real spacing. For the fraud vendor it's 40 ÷ 150 ms ≈ 267/s. Neither receiver was the limit: the fraud vendor's simulated server allows 500 at once, and analytics 1,000. Run 47's prediction ("about 800/s for analytics with 40 lanes") holds exactly.

How refusal happened. The backlog filled the fraud vendor's lanes. The first to reach its 40-slot limit blocked the dispatcher. The queue then filled behind it, and once it reached its limit, new deliveries were refused (t+9 to t+16 s). It's the same path as run 37's analytics refusals: one full lane stops intake for the whole endpoint, although other lanes still had room.

What the brief says about this endpoint

A refusal isn't the fraud vendor's policy

The brief's fraud vendor would rather an event be dropped than delivered more than 30 seconds late. So dropping fraud-vendor deliveries can be correct, but only by age: deliver what's fresh, discard what's older than 30 s. These 358 were refused at the door because a lane was full, when they were 0 seconds old. Meanwhile the deliveries that did get in waited up to 7.5 s, still well within 30 s. A staleness check would drop the right ones. The lane ceiling drops arbitrary ones.

Next

Lanes per endpoint, then the next step

  1. Give the two shared endpoints more lanes

    To carry the brief's 3,000/s, analytics needs at least 3,000 × 50 ms = 150 lanes, and the fraud vendor 1,060 × 150 ms ≈ 160, so about 300 each with headroom. Every other endpoint is fine with far fewer (merchant endpoints used 0–10). A lane count per endpoint (from registration, or by profile) gives the two busy ones about 300 for about 600 extra goroutines, instead of 300 lanes × 1,142 endpoints.

  2. Decide what happens when a lane fills

    Today one full lane blocks the dispatcher and the endpoint starts refusing. Options: let lanes hold more, have admission look at total waiting instead of the queue, and give the fraud vendor its brief-specified 30-second staleness drop.

  3. The generator for higher rates

    1 ms sleeps gave 750/s. Above that, send in batches per tick (for example 30 events every 10 ms). Before using a longer trace, read and send as you go instead of loading everything first: holdingchannel at 300,001 slots holds about 300,000 events in memory before sending starts.

  4. Then 1,200/s, then 3,000/s, with a longer trace

    15,000 events is only 5 s at 3,000/s. A 300,000-event trace generated with rate=3000 gives 100 s of sending.
